# Discovery — Product Vision & Thesis (living draft)

> Stage 1 · product-strategist artifact, captured live. Evolving; not final.

## The core reframe (Round 3 — pivotal)

The founders' real goal is **NOT** "consume learning resources consistently." It is to
**become capable enough to launch the marketing agency ASAP and start doing real client work.**
Learning is a *means*, not the end.

Their deepest failure mode is **tutorial hell**: watching content, feeling productive, and
never getting their hands dirty in the real world — learning a bit, then quitting.

What would genuinely motivate them: feeling that what they're learning is **immediately useful
in the real world** and is **actively moving them toward starting**.

### Design consequence (critical)
A naive, well-organized *learning library* is a tutorial-hell **enabler** — it optimizes
consumption. This product must do the opposite: it is an **execution engine that uses learning
as fuel**, not a resource library with reminders.

Implications to carry through the whole design:
- Learning items should connect to **real-world application / action**, not end at "watched."
- "Done" should lean toward **applied**, not merely **consumed**.
- Learning paths should **culminate in a real deliverable or action**, not a final video.
- The primary progress measure = **proximity to launch / real work done**, not hours learned.
- Anti-tutorial-hell mechanics belong at the core (bias toward doing, not hoarding).

## The two layers (Round 3 deepening)
The product spans two connected layers:
- **(A) Learn** — the knowledge/library layer: consume resources, build industry expertise.
- **(B) Build** — the execution layer: the concrete work of standing up their own agency —
  logo, branding, website, Instagram + other social pages, brand content, recruiting a
  **sales team**, etc. This is the "real work" that proves the learning and builds confidence.
The two are linked: learning fuels the build; the build grounds the learning in reality.

## Launch definition (from founders)
"Ready to take clients" = **team confidence + motivation** — deep industry knowledge plus the
agency's own assets in place. It is *subjective*, not a fixed checklist.

### Risk flagged (requirements-critic)
"Start once we feel confident" is a perfectionism trap — a second flavor of tutorial hell
where the team never feels ready. Mitigation to design in:
- **Ground confidence in evidence** — track tangible deliverables shipped and reps done, so
  "we feel ready" is backed by concrete proof.
- **Nudge toward starting small, real work early** rather than waiting for a perfect feeling.

## North star (candidate — to refine)
Launch-readiness over time — tangible agency deliverables shipped + skills *applied* + rising
(evidence-backed) team confidence — rather than resources consumed.

## Application model
Completion requiring an applied output is **optional per item**: some learning items have a
"do it for real" action attached; some are pure knowledge. Item-level property.

## Team & context
- 3 founders, equal peers, building a marketing agency. Pre-client phase.

## Scope decisions (Round 4)
- **Both layers are in-app.** The product tracks the learning library *and* the agency-build
  execution milestones (brand, site, socials, content, sales team) as work toward launch.
- **Confidence = milestones + self-rating, combined.** Tangible readiness milestones you check
  off, *plus* periodic self-rated confidence, rolling into a launch-readiness view.
- **Neutral on "start now" for v1.** The team decides when they're ready; no active push to
  start real work early. (Grounding confidence in evidence is fine — that's passive.)
